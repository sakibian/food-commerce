# Comparison: Chef Ready-Meals vs Parcel Staples (Business Models)

---

## 1. The Two Models

**Model A — Chef Ready-Meals (opencode's way)**
A commercial kitchen cooks daily organic meals; we deliver cooked food to the customer.

**Model B — Parcel Staples (your way)**
We source organic raw staples (oil, ghee, honey, rice, etc.), repack into pack/kg/bosta/bottle, and parcel to the customer. No chef, no cooking.

---

## 2. Side-by-Side Comparison

| Factor | Parcel Staples (Your way) | Chef Ready-Meals (My way) |
|---|---|---|
| Startup cost | Low — no kitchen/chef | High — kitchen + chef + staff |
| Time to launch | 2–4 weeks | 2–3 months |
| Operational risk | Low | High (daily cooking, spoilage) |
| Competition | High (Khaas, Organica, Sunnah) | Low — real gap |
| Margin | Medium | Higher |
| Customer LTV | Medium (reorders) | High (daily subscription) |
| Moat | Weak (reselling) | Strong (recipe/chef IP) |

---

## 3. Recommendation

**Start with Model B (Parcel Staples), scale into Model A later.**

Bangladesh's f-commerce market is huge and highly competitive — over 2.2 crore Facebook users in Dhaka and ~90,000 f-commerce orders/day. That is exactly why Phase 1 (parcel staples, low capex) is the safer entry before Phase 2 (chef meals).

Why:
- You are a small company — no chef/kitchen means fast, cheap launch and learning the zone.
- Research shows oil, ghee, honey, rice, tea are the MOST regularly reordered organic items — proven demand, low risk.
- It builds the two assets you need later anyway: trust + gated-community delivery route.

Once ~100–200 households reorder staples, layer in chef meals (Phase 2) — by then you know exactly which diet to cook.
